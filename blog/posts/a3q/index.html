This evening, while waiting for the M train to take me back to Brooklyn from dinner with my dad in Manhattan's so-called "Koreatown", I observed two identically formatted notifications of a temporary Q train restriction, written in English and Spanish respectively, posted upon a load-bearing pillar on the platform.

The English poster opened with the caption:

"No Q train between Brighton Beach and Stillwell Av. . ."

while the Spanish poster opened with:

"No habia servicio de la linea Q entre Brighton Beach y Stillwell Av. . ."

Why so many extra words for the Spanish version? El theorized it's because "Q" is a very common shorthand for "qué", which basically means "what" in Spanish. It's funny to imagine a rushed Hispanic commuter glance over at a sign to read:

"No WHAT between Brighton Beach and Stillwell Av. . ."

the old vs. the newgrass pt.1

#bluegrass #nyc

There's a joke oft repeated on web forums for the Berklee College Of Music; when a new or prospective student inquires to the digital either about who the disheveled figures seen murmuring around the wooden benches bolted to the main campus buildings are, the ether types back:

"Alumni."

When I learned the Guitar Center situated directly across Mass Ave from those benches had closed, I was astonished. I could hardly picture a more convenient location for any business, beat out narrowly by the Microcenter near MIT or any 7-11 next to any high school anywhere. Pondering the margins brought it into focus -- Guitar Center and other big box music stores make most of their money selling brand new yet scratched up Telecasters to idiots such as myself, hideously overpriced A/V equipment to dads with dreams, and $300 guitar lessons to my friend Meg. Berklee students have all that shit, and they're already paying $80,000 a year for music lessons.

So, to hell with the Mass Ave Guitar Center, it was selling waves to the ocean. I suspect replacement guitar string packs made up 90% of transactions, and today my shock has shifted to marveling that none of the dozens of cafes, restaurants, or bodegas bordering its now-empty storefront have put up a tiny window kiosk stuffed with Ernie Ball Regular Slinky Nickle Wounds. But, to hell with those businesses too (except for Pho Basil). The only spot I give any real weight to the cash flow of isn't across from Berklee, but up and around the corner, a few doors down on Boyston if you're facing the Fens.

(contd.)

I recently moved from a [Boston Triple Decker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-decker_%28house%29) into a converted warehouse loft building in Brooklyn called The Tea Factory. The source of this name is unclear -- a [NYT article from 2008](https://archive.is/SpChX#selection-551.109-551.138) claims the building was once the property of "Spice and Tea Exclusively Ltd", an entity with no search results besides the aforementioned article.

It's a beautiful apartment with white walls, very tall ceilings, and a hallway stretching perpendicular to the street for so long and so wide it might as well be called a hall. The bedrooms run along the hall railroad style and the unit is in the center of the building, so we possess but two windows to the outside world -- one which marks the end of our glorious hall, and another six feet to its right in the "front" bedroom. Every bedroom has windows, though. In a fit of optimism, whoever assembled our chateau gave every room a window into each room it's adjacent to along with a window to the hall (spare the bathroom). It's a bizarre arrangement, one which doesn't serve much of a purpose nor do anyone any good. 

I sleep in the bedroom farthest from the sun. If I don't have any lights on and the door is closed, it's almost pitch black. Some morning light trickles in thanks to my bizarre neighborly windows, but not enough to simulate a gentle sunrise. What it does simulate, however, is the sensation of waking up after laying down for a catnap at 4:15pm and waking up 20 quick minutes later but where's the sun? and you're sweaty and sooo bewildered and here's the cheerful chatter of your roommates washing their dinner dishware wafting in from the kitchen -- they do all of their dishes right after they finish eating (and if something's burnt, it's left to soak in warm soapy water) -- do you? -- the day's over, you blew it, shut your eyes and try again tomorrow. To mitigate the waking nightmare, I went to a hardware store and bought some timer outlets. Now, my bedside lamps switch on automatically at 7:30am, setting the stage for 7:45 sharp when [WBGO 88.3FM](https://www.wbgo.org/) blasts brashly from my radio alarm clock in an arc through the hoop balancing on the nose of the little wire seal sculpture perched at the foot of my bed and into my perforated eardrums no matter how gingerly I wind the volume knob towards 179° every previous evening. I set a lamp timer for the kitchen as well, as meekly fumbling through a shadowy kitchen and flipping various levers and switches to make the shadows go away every morning made me feel like a glum prisoner on an outer space mining colony.

At my previous apartment in Boston, I spent far too much time in my bedroom, working a very unpleasant full-time remote software engineer job. Every morning, I would wake up at 8am, make a feeble attempt to get up to work out or shower, and be coaxed back into sleep by my warm drowsy girlfriend. 55 minutes later, it's go time -- cram morning updates from India before Team Stupid logs off, join morning standup to get yelled at by the CEO's dad (???), then endeavor to get 3 or 4 hours of productive work done before being assigned to "own" any amount of URGENT tasks, piled on from 9am to midnight. The only reason it was bearable was because my room was huge and airy and got gorgeous light at all times of the day. My plants flourished under the glow of my stained glass hangers and sometimes the sunrise would beam into my bright red wooden office chair so fiercely that a bloody phantasm would come alive and dance across the walls, careening from vine to vine. I never took a picture.

Flipper gray is a dolphin-forward colloquial term for the dull gray paint which landlords and house flippers gleefully douse apartment walls in, having found the perfect shade to minimize sratchy upkeep and maximize their lowest common denominator. Often, they leave just the windowshades and door frames intact -- sparse taunting lines of eggshells and alabaster, salt and chiffon, cream and snow.
